\section{Concluding Remarks}
\label{sec:conclusions}

\subsection{Key Conclusions}
\label{subsec:key_conclusions}

This paper has established an arbitrary-order Hu--Zhang mixed finite element framework for density-based topology optimization. The main conclusions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Arbitrary-order Hu--Zhang TO framework}: Within the Hellinger--Reissner framework with non-homogeneous traction boundaries, the essential traction condition is imposed through a design-independent lifting field; the lifting term makes the discrete right-hand side depend on the material distribution, and the consistent adjoint sensitivities of the complementary-energy objective and of the local stress constraints account for this dependence. Through the management of normal-trace degrees of freedom, local corner relaxation and jump stabilization for lower orders, the stabilized formulations with $k\le d$ and the native formulations with $k\ge d+1$ run within the same optimization workflow. The observed convergence orders in the manufactured-solution tests agree with the theoretical estimates; in the clamped beam benchmark, the two discretizations yield similar load-carrying layouts and close compliances under a common reanalysis.
    \item \textbf{Topology optimization under nearly incompressible conditions}: The compliance bilinear form of the Hu--Zhang elements remains bounded as $\lambda\to\infty$; no $\lambda$-uniform discrete stability estimate is established in this paper, and the locking resistance is examined numerically. In the bearing benchmark, the Hu--Zhang elements exhibit similar fully solid compliance convergence trends for the two Poisson's ratios, without the pronounced loss of accuracy observed for linear displacement elements; the common reanalysis shows that, in the nearly incompressible group, the designs obtained with Hu--Zhang elements have lower compliance than those obtained with linear displacement elements and are close to those obtained with quadratic displacement elements.
    \item \textbf{Local stress-constrained TO driven by independent stresses}: The Hu--Zhang stress field belongs to $H(\operatorname{div},\Omega;\mathbb S)$, so its normal traction is single-valued across elements in the trace sense. The local von Mises stress constraints are constructed directly from this primary stress variable and combined with a density-dependent relaxed threshold and the ALM--MMA procedure to solve the volume minimization problem, without recovering stresses from displacement gradients. In the cantilever benchmark, HZMFEM $k=3$ and LFEM $p=3$ yield similar layouts and stress distributions, HZMFEM $k=2,3,4$ all reach the stopping criterion, with the constraints evaluated on the criterion set by each discretization; the element-wise traction jumps of the Hu--Zhang stress fields of all three orders are at round-off level, whereas those of the stresses recovered from displacement gradients are clearly non-zero.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Future Work and Outlook}
\label{subsec:future_work}

The numerical study of this paper is restricted to two-dimensional small-strain linear elasticity. Future work will proceed along the following three directions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Three-dimensional extension and parallel solvers.} Extend the local corner relaxation and the treatment of boundary conditions to three-dimensional Hu--Zhang discretizations, and study the organization of stress degrees of freedom on edges and vertices where boundaries meet, so that prescribed traction conditions remain compatible with inter-element normal-trace continuity. To address the growth of unknowns for higher-order discretizations and the solution difficulties caused by material stiffness contrast, study block preconditioners and parallel solvers for the mixed saddle-point systems, and reuse solver information between successive optimization steps to reduce the computational cost.
    \item \textbf{Error-driven mesh and order adaptivity.} Develop a posteriori error estimates for compliance and local stress constraints, and apply local mesh refinement and order adjustment according to the corresponding accuracy requirements, concentrating computational resources on stress concentration regions and material transition regions. Meanwhile, study the transfer of design variables across mesh changes and the coordinated treatment of filtering and projection to keep the design length scale consistent.
    \item \textbf{Finite deformation and nonlinear materials.} For nearly incompressible hyperelastic structures, develop stress--displacement mixed discretizations compatible with finite-deformation kinematics, together with the corresponding material interpolation and consistent adjoint sensitivities. To address the large deformation and mesh distortion in low-density regions, study material regularization and nonlinear solution strategies to improve the robustness of the optimization process, and assess stress accuracy, constraint satisfaction and computational cost through benchmarks at different load levels and material parameters.
\end{enumerate}
